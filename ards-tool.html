ARDS Management Protocol

Critical Care Mechanical Ventilation & Rescue Triage

1. ARDS Diagnosis & Severity Stratification (Berlin Definition)
Evaluate PaO2/FiO2 ratio on PEEP ≥ 5 cmH2O with bilateral infiltrates not fully explained by heart failure.

Mild ARDS: Low Tidal Volume Strategy

  • Low Tidal Volume (LTVV): Target 4–8 mL/kg Predicted Body Weight (PBW). Initial target: 6 mL/kg PBW.
  • Pressure Limit: Maintain Plateau Pressure (Pplat) ≤ 30 cmH2O.
  • PEEP Titration: Use Low PEEP / Higher FiO2 titration table (PEEP 5–10 cmH2O).
  • Fluid Management: Conservative fluid strategy once hemodynamically stable (Aim for net neutral/negative).
2. Moderate ARDS Management
Implement LTVV (4–8 mL/kg PBW) + High PEEP Strategy. Assess P/F ratio after optimization.

Moderate ARDS: Optimization

  • Ventilation: Continue 6 mL/kg PBW; keep Pplat ≤ 30 cmH2O and Driving Pressure (ΔP = Pplat - PEEP) < 14 cmH2O.
  • PEEP: Higher PEEP strategy (10–14 cmH2O).
  • Sedation: Light-to-moderate sedation; minimize neuromuscular blockades unless dyssynchrony occurs.
2. Severe ARDS / Refractory Hypoxemia Protocol (P/F ≤ 150)
Immediate escalation for severe ARDS failing baseline lung-protective ventilation.

Prone Positioning & Neuromuscular Blockade

  • Prone Positioning: Minimum 16 consecutive hours per day in prone position.
  • NMBA: Short-term continuous cisatracurium infusion (up to 48 hours) for severe patient-ventilator dyssynchrony or refractory driving pressures.
  • Ventilator Targets: Target PaO2 55–80 mmHg (SpO2 88–95%); allow Permissive Hypercapnia (pH ≥ 7.20).
3. Advanced Mechanics: Driving Pressure (ΔP) Assessment
Calculate ΔP = Plateau Pressure - PEEP. Target ΔP < 14–15 cmH2O.

Elevated Driving Pressure Management

  • If ΔP > 15 cmH2O: Reduce Vt by 0.5–1 mL/kg PBW (down to 4 mL/kg).
  • Recruitment: Titrate PEEP to maximize compliance without overdistending healthy alveoli.
  • Permissive Hypercapnia: Accept elevated PaCO2 as long as pH remains ≥ 7.20 (use sodium bicarbonate infusion only if severe acidemia threatens hemodynamics).
4. Refractory Hypoxemia & Advanced Rescue Interventions
P/F < 80 despite proning, high PEEP, and NMBA. Select rescue therapy:

Pulmonary Vasodilator Therapy

  • Agents: Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO 5–20 ppm) or Inhaled Epoprostenol (PGI2).
  • Mechanism: Selective pulmonary vasodilation in ventilated lung units to reduce V/Q mismatch.
  • Note: Used primarily as a bridge to VV-ECMO or stabilization; does not improve overall mortality.

Veno-Venous Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (VV-ECMO)

  • EOLIA Indications: PaO2/FiO2 < 50 for > 3 hours, OR PaO2/FiO2 < 80 for > 6 hours, OR pH < 7.15 with PaCO2 ≥ 60 mmHg despite optimal mechanical ventilation.
  • Ultra-Lung-Protective Ventilation: On ECMO, drop Vt to 2–3 mL/kg PBW, Pplat ≤ 24 cmH2O, RR 4–10/min to promote lung rest.
  • Cannulation: Dual-lumen single cannula (Femoral-Jugular) or bi-femoral setup.
