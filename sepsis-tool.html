Sepsis & Septic Shock — Rapid Management Tool (Adults)

Concise resuscitation checklist: fluids, antibiotics, vasopressors, lactate, source control, and bundle timing (based on 2026 guideline summaries).

Updated: 2026

Identify

Suspect sepsis in any patient with infection + organ dysfunction (SOFA rise, hypotension, lactate >2 mmol/L).

Immediate (First hour) — "Sepsis Bundle"

  • Measure lactate immediately; remeasure if elevated (>2 mmol/L).
  • Obtain blood cultures (≥2 sets) before antibiotics — but do not delay antibiotics beyond ~45 minutes.
  • Administer broad‑spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour of recognition.
  • If hypotensive or lactate ≥4 mmol/L → give 30 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid (rapid boluses 500–1000 mL) while reassessing responsiveness.
  • If persistent hypotension after initial fluids → start norepinephrine to achieve MAP ≥65 mmHg.

Fluids & Fluid Responsiveness

  • Use balanced crystalloids (e.g., Plasma-Lyte, Ringer’s) over normal saline when available.
  • Avoid HES. Consider albumin if large volumes of crystalloids are required.
  • Guide further fluids by dynamic measures (passive leg raise, stroke volume variation, capillary refill, bedside ultrasound).

Vasopressors & Hemodynamic Targets

  • Norepinephrine is first-line vasopressor.
  • Target MAP ≥65 mmHg for most adults; consider higher target (70–85 mmHg) for patients with chronic hypertension.
  • Add vasopressin or epinephrine if norepinephrine alone insufficient; minimize dose and wean as tolerated.

Monitoring & Lactate

  • Serial lactate every 2–4 hours until normalization to assess response to resuscitation.
  • Use bedside perfusion assessments (capillary refill, urine output, mental status) alongside hemodynamics.

Source Control & Antimicrobial Stewardship

  • Perform source control (drain abscess, remove infected device) as soon as practical — aim within 6–12 hours when feasible.
  • Start empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics early; narrow therapy based on cultures and stewardship principles.

Corticosteroids

Consider IV hydrocortisone (e.g., 200 mg/day) for adults with refractory septic shock despite adequate fluids and vasopressors.

Documentation & Team Response

  • Activate a sepsis huddle/team if local pathway exists; document time of recognition, antibiotics, fluids, and vasopressor start.
  • Review daily for de-escalation opportunities and source control progress.
This tool summarizes major guideline recommendations (2026) and is not a substitute for clinical judgment. Please follow local protocols and consult specialists as needed.
